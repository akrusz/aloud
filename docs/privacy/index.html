Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 15, 2026

aloud is built to be private, and to give you a choice about where your data goes. You can run it locally with your own providers, so nothing touches us at all; or you can use aloud cloud, our optional hosted service that connects you to AI and voice providers without us keeping your sessions. This policy explains what happens to your data in each case.

The short version

  • aloud uses functionality like LLMs, speech-to-text, and text-to-speech. If your device supports these functions locally, you may use it to keep that data fully on-device. If you bring your own API keys or subscription, your content goes only to the providers you choose. If you use aloud cloud, we list below which provider runs each option, along with the ones we use for background functionality.
  • We don't sell your data; we don't use it for advertising; we don't use your conversations to train AI. We don't retain your conversations at all when you do use aloud cloud.
  • aloud cloud needs an account and credits because it costs us money to run; for that we process the minimum required: sign-in details, your credit balance and usage, and payments handled by our payment processor.
  • A conversation history stored only on your device is enabled by default, but you can turn it off in Settings.

Where your data goes

Sessions use three main capabilities: speech recognition turns your audio into text, the facilitator answers your words and the conversation so far, and a voice speaks its reply. Each part runs where you choose:

  • On your device (Whisper, Ollama, Piper, your system's voices): nothing leaves it.
  • A provider you connect (with your own API key, e.g. Anthropic, OpenAI, OpenRouter, Venice, Groq, or your Claude subscription via the claude CLI): your content goes straight from your device to that provider. These don't require an aloud account, and we receive none of your content unless you turn on Voice commands (below).
  • Your platform or browser. The mobile apps can use your phone's built-in speech recognizer, and browsers may offer their own recognizer and online voices (Chrome's Google voices, for example). These abide by the platform's own privacy policy, and may use their servers. We never receive it.
  • aloud cloud, our optional hosted service, which works "out of the box" on every platform regardless of device functionality. For devices without local functionality, it will be the only option. It relays your audio and text to the provider of the model you pick (see who runs each model) and returns the result.

aloud cloud never stores your session content. Your audio, your words and the AI's replies pass through only to reach the provider, and are discarded. We never record your audio.

The microphone also listens on the setup screen so you can say "begin" or change settings. The mic icon beside Begin shows when it's on. It closes by itself after hearing talk that isn't meant for it, and on aloud cloud or a phone after a couple of idle minutes. It can be switched off in Settings (Voice control on the setup screen). What it hears takes the same path as speech recognition configured for sessions.

Voice commands and background functionality

On aloud cloud, short things you say (sometimes with a few recent lines from you or the facilitator) also go to a small classification model, to recognize spoken commands ("set a timer for ten minutes"), tell when you're calling the facilitator back from a silence, and notice if you might be frightened or unwell so the facilitator can react appropriately. That last check sometimes runs on a language model instead. On the setup screen, when you're signed in, a short request the app can't match on your device is checked the same way.

Locally or with your own keys these checks are off by default. Turning on Voice commands in Settings (a free aloud account, no credits) sends them through aloud cloud to the same model, even with an on-device facilitator; your conversation still goes only where it did before, and your API keys are never part of it. In the desktop app you can instead add your own TypeSafe key, which sends them straight from your device to TypeSafe, with no aloud account.

What stays on your device

Saved transcripts and summaries, your settings, and any API keys you enter stay on your device however you use aloud, with no cloud sync; delete them whenever you like. Three requests leave it without session content: update checks, downloads of on-device models (from Hugging Face or Ollama), and, in the web and mobile apps, looking up a provider's model list - which is relayed through our server with your key, used only in memory, and not stored or logged. The desktop app makes that lookup directly.

What aloud cloud keeps

None of this includes anything you said or the AI replied. aloud uses no third-party analytics or behavioral tracking. We do have error logging and usage data that never carry user text or audio.

  • Account. Your sign-in: Google or Apple (which share a basic identifier such as your email address), or an email address and password.
  • Email updates, only if you tick the box at signup. Turn them off any time on the account page. We never share or sell your address.
  • Credits and usage. Your balance and metered usage (audio and text processed, per-session totals such as turn count), for billing, our costs and abuse prevention. It isn't behavioral profiling.
  • Payments. Card payments go through Stripe, and we never see your full card details. A stablecoin payment is recorded on a public blockchain by nature; we receive only what's needed to credit your account.
  • Abuse prevention. The IP address you signed up from, deleted after 90 days or when you delete your account. Free credits are given once per person, so we keep a one-way hash of the email address (for some grants, the sign-in ID) that received them, never the address itself, including after you delete your account.
  • Logs and error records. Short-lived technical logs for diagnostics and security, and, when a relayed call or a credit purchase fails, a record of what went wrong (error code, provider, model, token or byte counts), so we notice problems the app recovers from without showing you.

Deleting your account removes your sign-in identities and email-list entry and scrubs your email address and IP from the record. What remains is an anonymous credit ledger and error records, kept for accounting, and the free-credit hashes.

aloud cloud runs on Fly.io in the United States, so your account information and the content we relay are processed there wherever you are, the EU and UK included; providers may process data in other regions. The account database is backed up to Cloudflare R2, and data removed from it ages out of the backup within about a day. Both serve us as infrastructure providers under data processing agreements.

Third-party providers

Any AI or voice provider that processes your content, whether you connect it or aloud cloud relays to it, does so under its own privacy policy and terms, and some keep data for a limited time; aloud is not responsible for how they handle it.

Whichever facilitator you pick, aloud cloud also sends parts of the session to Claude Haiku (Anthropic) and GPT-5 nano (OpenAI) for summarization, and some of the checks above. Through OpenRouter we allow only hosts that don't train on what they receive. Inworld's zero-data-retention setting isn't yet on for our account (we've asked), so until it is, Inworld may keep the reply text it voices; pick another voice to avoid that.

Your rights

You can see, correct, export or delete the account data we hold, and object to or restrict our use of it. Deleting your account from the app's account screen takes effect at once (details); email us for anything else. In the EU, UK or another region with a data protection law, these are your rights under it, and you can also complain to your local data protection authority. Our legal bases are performing our contract with you (accounts, credits, relaying your sessions) and our legitimate interest in keeping the service secure and preventing abuse.

Children

aloud is not directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age in your jurisdiction), and we do not knowingly collect information from them.
